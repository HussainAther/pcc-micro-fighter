# Frozen retreat-backfire result — v0.8.0

The v0.8 diagnostic compares the accepted v0.5 Family B Control policy with the rejected v0.7 sustained-threat retreat/defense rule under the same Family B Pressure policy and balanced player orders.

## Result

The v0.7 policy again performs substantially worse:

- v0.5 Pressure decisive win rate: **0.8244**
- v0.7 Pressure decisive win rate: **0.9767**
- v0.5 Control mean health margin: **-1.2513**
- v0.7 Control mean health margin: **-2.5325**

Across **2,343** v0.7 retreat-trigger events:

- initiative-forfeiture proxy rate: **0.8801**
- retreat ineffectiveness rate: **0.7998**
- successful distance-gain rate: **0.2002**
- immediate Pressure re-entry after successful gain: **0.8785**
- two-tick displacement persistence after successful gain: **0.0107**
- mean damage received over the next two ticks: **0.6611**

All four prespecified descriptive mechanism checks were triggered.

## Interpretation

The rejected rule does not fail merely because retreat is too conservative. In this arena it usually fails to create meaningful separation, and even successful separation is almost immediately erased by Pressure. At the same time, the retreat trigger frequently occurs in states where Control plausibly has offensive initiative available.

The strongest conclusion is therefore that spatial Control should **not** be operationalized as simple threat-avoidance or distance maximization. A more plausible structural interpretation is value-sensitive regulation of space and initiative: disengage only when separation can persist or when the value of yielding initiative is justified.

This is a retained negative intervention result. No v0.8 policy change was made and construct recovery remains blocked.
